# Project 1 validation plan and proposed Project 2 experiment

Project 1 focuses on whether the stated model ingredients are coherent and whether data collected from the live game support their assumptions. It does **not** yet provide a controlled ranking of the three policies. Project 2 is the proposed, not-yet-started same-environment comparison. The live game's random-seed mechanism and any ability to replay identical scenarios are unverified.

## Causal information and data records

Log one immutable decision snapshot **before** each action: game ID, timestamp, rules version, model name/version, seat and round, balance, historical peak, drawdown, visible ranks and count, quote, public bot state, available capital, legal actions, posterior and its version, suggested action and quantity, estimated terminal distribution, dynamic λ or preservation priorities, and explanation. After settlement, append actual executed action, actual fills, revealed cards (tagged *post-settlement only*), realized P&L, penalties, final round balance, and whether the recommendation was followed. At game end record final balance, place, model assignment, missingness, and Oracle results in distinct fields. Actual hidden ranks are outcome labels, never live features.

The existing recorder captures some of these items but not a complete immutable decision snapshot or model version. Its CSV can seed descriptive analysis after validating face-up counts and round completeness. Do not silently turn incomplete or interrupted games into finished-session samples; retain an explicit exclusion reason and report exclusions by model.

## Calibrate, then freeze

Use complete *earlier* games for fitting card likelihoods, joint bot responses, λ coefficients, preservation threshold $d_*$, and numerical tie tolerance. Split by **game**, not round, because the four rounds share bankroll and opponents. Reserve later untouched games for evaluation. Compare uniform and without-replacement rank models with held-out log score and calibration, including conditioning on visible count. Add quote-conditioned posteriors only if held-out predictive improvement survives control for public cards, round and maker identity. Maker fills must be modeled jointly by round; three bot trades in one round are not three independent samples. Out-of-support candidate quotes receive no claim of reliable expected value.

For Project 1 live games, collect complete records to check dealing, quote, collateral, and bot-response assumptions and to calibrate provisional model ingredients. If exploratory performance is reported, preassign one frozen policy to each whole game, rotate assignments over time, and record deviations. Different live games may have different card deals, visible patterns, quotes, bot reactions, and seats. Unknown seed control means they are **not matched trials**; unpaired comparisons are descriptive and cannot establish which policy wins under identical conditions. A paired counterfactual Taker payoff at the *observed* settlement can be calculated for alternative legal quantities at that state, but future bankroll paths and Maker fills cannot be read from that single outcome.

Project 2 would construct and validate an environment with explicit control over exogenous random inputs, then evaluate all three frozen policies using the same initial seat, rules, card-generation mechanism, visibility mask, and opponent-response mechanism on paired seeds. Bot fills and later legal quantities must be recomputed after each model's action, so same seed does not imply identical downstream states. Separate training from held-out evaluation seeds and report simulator-to-live discrepancies before making claims about the live game. This environment and comparison have **not** been built.

## Endpoints and uncertainty

Primary outcomes: mean final profit for Profit Maximization; mean final profit together with mean/MDD and downside severity for Risk-Adjusted; $P(\min B_r<400)$ followed by $P(B_4<500)$ for Capital Preservation. Secondary outcomes for all: median profit, initial-capital return, profit SD, downside deviation, MDD, $P(B_4<400)$, worst 5% outcomes/CVaR when estimable, participation, invalid orders, penalties, and timeouts. Report confidence intervals clustered by game and keep seat-stratified results. Predefine a practically meaningful difference before collecting evaluation games; choose sample size from desired interval width or power under pilot variance, not a guessed fixed count. Rare 400 breaches may need far more games than mean-profit estimation. A zero observed breach frequency is not proof of zero risk.

Hypotheses for the future controlled comparison, not Project 1 conclusions: H1 Profit has the highest mean but more downside; H2 Risk-Adjusted lowers drawdown while retaining material mean profit; H3 Capital Preservation lowers floor/principal breach frequency; H4 a calibrated dynamic λ improves over fixed λ. Report failed hypotheses and sensitivity to calibration choices. Use visible-count, quote-spread, posterior-uncertainty and Maker/Taker slices only with adequate game-level counts; avoid selecting a winning slice after viewing results.

## Offline Oracle definitions

**Full-Information Oracle:** after import, reveal the current round's hidden cards when evaluating that decision. It still does not know future cards/quotes/fills. For Taker it can optimize a legal current action under known $V$ and a specified continuation model. For Maker it still needs a response model for counterfactual quotes. Its advantage measures the value of *current* hidden information under stated assumptions.

**Perfect-Foresight Oracle:** after import, may know the entire future exogenous card/quote sequence, but changing an action can change future bankroll and legal sizes; changing a Maker quote can change bot fills. A valid counterfactual replay therefore needs a structural response/transition law and re-evaluates legality and bankroll after every hypothetical action. Simply swapping a trade into the recorded outcome is not a four-round optimum. Report information set, search method, version, and approximation limits; use “optimal” only if the search truly optimizes the stated legal problem. Neither Oracle's features enter normal-policy decisions or training features. Oracle analysis occurs after the completed-game import, without changing the live extension in this research phase.
